Tech layoffs in 2026 have already passed the full-year total for 2025, but how far past depends on who is counting. If you are trying to understand the job market, it helps to know why the headline numbers disagree.

## Four trackers, four totals

| Tracker | 2026 count | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| [Layoffs.fyi](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/tech-layoffs-statistics/) | 128,536 employees at 299 companies | Through Sept 10 |
| [Challenger, Gray & Christmas](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/tech-layoffs-statistics/) | 155,126 US tech job cuts | Through August; up 52% year over year |
| [TrueUp](https://www.trueup.io/layoffs) | 190,077 people across 623 layoffs | Running total at time of writing |
| [SkillSyncer](https://startupfortune.com/tech-layoffs-top-225000-in-2026-as-companies-blame-the-same-ai-theyre-funding/) | 225,122 workers across 519 events | As of late September |

For comparison, Layoffs.fyi counted 122,606 tech layoffs for all of 2025, so its 2026 total had already passed that by September, as [Second Talent's summary](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/tech-layoffs-statistics/) notes.

## Why the numbers differ

Trackers draw the line around "tech" differently. Startup Fortune describes Layoffs.fyi as covering a narrower slice, mostly venture-backed and public tech companies, which helps explain why it reports the lowest figure. Challenger counts announced job cuts in the US, a different measure again. Always check what a tracker includes before quoting its number.

## The AI attribution problem

Many cuts are now explained with AI. Challenger data cited by [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3816579/tech-layoffs-this-year-a-timeline.html) shows AI blamed for 40% of tech cuts in May, up from 7% in January. Second Talent reports that Layoffs.fyi tags 72% of 2026 layoffs as AI-related, versus 29% in 2025, but that tag applies whenever the company or press attributed a cut to AI, including cuts made to fund AI investment.

That distinction matters. A layoff linked to AI spending is not the same as a job an AI system took over. The [SkillSyncer tracker](https://skillsyncer.com/layoffs-tracker) itself acknowledges that some companies blame AI for cuts they would have made anyway, while others are genuinely driven by AI adoption.

## Big names and what to watch

Oracle leads the 2026 list with 21,000 cuts, followed by Amazon at 17,267, according to a [TradingView News roundup](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:d3e6e0d638aef:0-tech-layoffs-2026-as-ai-takes-center-stage-here-s-a-list-of-major-companies-that-laid-off-employees/). Computerworld's timeline also records Meta cutting 8,000 jobs in May. [Tech Times reports](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328106/20260928/tech-layoffs-top-225000-oracle-borrowed-billions-ai-then-cut-workers-pay-it.htm) that California's proposed SB 951 would require 90 days' notice before AI-driven layoffs and disclosure of the AI systems involved; the bill is still pending.

## My take

I suspect AI is partly an excuse. Many companies hired heavily during the COVID boom and probably ended up with more engineers than work, and "we're replacing roles with AI" sounds better to investors than admitting a correction. I can't know what any executive is thinking, and some cuts, like those made to pay for AI spending, really are tied to AI. But I'd treat "AI did it" with skepticism until a company shows what AI actually took over.
